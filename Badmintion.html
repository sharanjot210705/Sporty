🏆 INTRODUCTION TO BADMINTON

Badminton is a dynamic racket sport played with shuttlecocks across a net. Whether in singles or doubles, it combines explosive speed, precision, and breathtaking agility. The shuttlecock, often called a "birdie," can reach speeds over 400 km/h, making badminton the fastest racket sport in the world.

Originating from the ancient game of battledore and shuttlecock, modern badminton became an Olympic sport in 1992. Today, it thrives globally with thrilling competitions like the All England Open, BWF World Championships, and the Olympic Games. Players require exceptional stamina, wrist flexibility, and tactical intelligence.

🏅 Olympic action – mixed doubles final

Points are scored by landing the shuttlecock inside the opponent’s court. Matches are played in a best-of-three games format (21 points each). The sport demands supreme fitness: anaerobic endurance, lightning reflexes, and core strength. From backyard leisure to professional arena, badminton unites millions across Asia, Europe, and the Americas.

📖 HISTORY OF BADMINTON

The roots of badminton stretch back centuries across Eurasia with games using a shuttlecock. However, the modern version emerged in British India during the 19th century, where military officers added a net and competitive rules. The game was known as "Poona" (after the city of Pune) before gaining popularity in England.

In 1873, the Duke of Beaufort introduced the sport to his guests at Badminton House in Gloucestershire – giving the game its enduring name. By 1893, the Badminton Association of England published the first official rules, and the All England Open Championships began in 1899, still one of the most prestigious tournaments today.

The International Badminton Federation (now BWF) was founded in 1934. While Denmark dominated European play for decades, Asian nations — China, Indonesia, Malaysia, South Korea, Japan, and India — have risen as powerhouses. Badminton became a full Olympic medal sport in Barcelona 1992, and mixed doubles was added in 1996. Today, it’s one of Asia’s most beloved sports with record-breaking viewership.

🏸 Did you know? The fastest badminton smash recorded is 426 km/h (264.7 mph) by Malaysia's Tan Boon Heong.

📏 OFFICIAL RULES & REGULATIONS

Based on the BWF Laws of Badminton, here are the essential rules that shape every match.

🏟️ Court Dimensions

The court is 13.4 meters long. For singles, width is 5.18 m; for doubles, it’s 6.1 m. The net stands 1.55 m at the edges and 1.524 m at the center. A clear space above the court is mandatory for competitive play.

🎯 Serving Rules

🚫 Below waist
The shuttle must be struck below 1.15 meters from the surface.
🔄 Diagonal service
Server and receiver stand in opposite service courts.
⚡ Even = right
When score is even, serve from right court; odd from left court (singles).
👥 Doubles rotation
Only one serve attempt; service rotates between partners after winning a rally.

🏅 Scoring System

Matches are played to 21 points (rally scoring). If the score reaches 20-all, a player must lead by 2 points to win, up to a maximum of 30 points. A match consists of the best of three games. Players change ends after the first game and, if a third game is played, at 11 points.

Lets are called for unforeseen disturbances or if the receiver is not ready. The rally is replayed without changing the score.

🛠️ EQUIPMENT & GEAR

High-quality gear defines performance. Professional players customize every element – from string tension to grip thickness.

🏸 Rackets

Carbon fibre composite, 70–95g. Stiff frames deliver power and control. Isometric head shapes enlarge the sweet spot.

🎗️ Strings

Thin gauges (0.62–0.73mm) with tensions 18–36 lbs. Pro players prefer high tension (26–32 lbs) for precision.

✋ Grips

PU synthetic or towel grips for sweat absorption. Overgrips allow frequent changes without damaging the handle.

🏑 Shuttlecock

16 feathers embedded in cork base — speed up to 400 km/h. Nylon shuttles offer durability for recreational play.

🎯 Additional Gear

Badminton shoes with gum rubber soles ensure excellent grip on wooden courts. Wristbands, headbands, and court bags complete the professional setup. Advanced players also use vibration dampeners and custom weighting systems.

Choosing the right equipment elevates your game. Beginners should opt for lightweight, balanced rackets, while competitive players explore head-heavy or head-light frames depending on playing style (offensive vs. defensive).

🏆 INTRODUCTION TO CRICKET

Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of eleven players on a circular or oval-shaped field. At the center lies a 22-yard pitch with wickets at each end. The game blends explosive power, tactical genius, and timeless tradition. With over 2.5 billion fans worldwide, cricket is the second most popular sport on the planet.

Matches range from the traditional five-day Test cricket — the ultimate examination of skill and endurance — to the high-octane T20 format where games finish in about three hours. The ICC Cricket World Cup, T20 World Cup, and iconic rivalries like The Ashes (England vs Australia) captivate audiences across continents.

🏏 High-voltage cricket action — players in full flow

The objective: score more runs than the opposition. A batsman hits the ball and runs between the wickets, while the bowling side aims to dismiss batters through bowled, caught, LBW, or run-out. Cricket demands athleticism, sharp reflexes, and deep strategy — captains set fields, rotate bowlers, and adjust tactics over hours or days.

📖 HISTORY OF CRICKET

Cricket originated in south-east England during the 16th century. The earliest definite reference dates from 1598. By the 18th century, it became England's national sport. The Hambledon Club and later Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) — founded in 1787 — became the custodians of the Laws of Cricket.

The first international match took place in 1844 between USA and Canada. However, the historic 1877 Test match between England and Australia at the Melbourne Cricket Ground marked the birth of Test cricket. The rivalry gave rise to The Ashes in 1882, one of sport's greatest traditions.

Cricket expanded with the British Empire, taking root in India, Australia, West Indies, South Africa, and New Zealand. One Day Internationals (ODIs) emerged in the 1970s, with the first Cricket World Cup held in 1975. The revolutionary T20 format arrived in 2003, culminating in the first ICC T20 World Cup in 2007. Today, franchise leagues like the Indian Premier League (IPL) combine sport and entertainment, attracting global superstars.

🏆 Iconic moments: Kapil Dev's 1983 World Cup triumph, Sachin Tendulkar's 100 international centuries, and Ben Stokes' 2019 World Cup heroics.

📏 CRICKET RULES & LAWS

Governed by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) and International Cricket Council (ICC), cricket's laws ensure fair play across all formats.

🏏 Basic Structure

🎯 11 players each
Two teams, one bats while the other bowls and fields. Innings end after 10 wickets or overs limit.
🔄 Runs & wickets
Batsmen score runs by running or hitting boundaries (4 or 6 runs). Bowling side aims to dismiss batters.
⚖️ Umpires & DRS
Two on-field umpires, third umpire for reviews. Decision Review System (DRS) adds technology.

📌 Dismissal Methods

🎯 Bowled
Ball hits stumps, dislodging bails.
🧤 Caught
Batsman hits ball, fielder catches before it touches ground.
🦵 LBW
Leg Before Wicket — ball hits pad in line with stumps.
🏃 Run out
Fielders hit stumps before batsman completes run.
⚡ Stumped
Wicketkeeper removes bails when batter out of crease.

📊 Scoring System

Runs are accumulated through running between wickets (1-3 runs) or hitting boundaries: 4 runs if ball bounces before crossing rope, 6 runs if it clears the boundary on the full. Extras (no-balls, wides, byes) add to the total. The team with more runs wins.

🛠️ CRICKET EQUIPMENT

From willow bats to protective gear, cricket equipment blends tradition with cutting-edge innovation.

🏏 Bat & Ball

Willow bat (English or Kashmir) with curved face. Leather ball with seam — red for Tests, white for limited-overs, pink for day-night.

🎯 Stumps & Bails

Three stumps (28 inches tall) with two bails on top. LED stumps with zing bails used in modern cricket.

🛡️ Protection

Helmet with grille, chest guard, arm guard, thigh pad, gloves, and abdominal guard (box). Essential for safety.

👟 Footwear

Spiked shoes for grip on turf. Rubber-soled shoes for artificial or indoor surfaces.

📡 Modern Innovations

Technology enhances the sport: Snickometer, Hawk-Eye, Hot Spot aid umpiring decisions. Players use lightweight carbon-fiber bats, advanced helmet designs, and smart sensors for performance analysis. The Kookaburra, Dukes, and SG are premier ball manufacturers across regions.

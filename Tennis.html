🏆 INTRODUCTION TO TENNIS

Tennis is a timeless racquet sport played between two individuals (singles) or two pairs (doubles). The objective is to strike the ball over a net into the opponent's court, making it impossible to return. Combining explosive movement, pinpoint accuracy, and mental resilience, tennis is one of the world's most elegant yet physically demanding sports.

From the pristine lawns of Wimbledon to the electric atmosphere of the US Open, tennis captivates millions. The ATP (men) and WTA (women) tours feature icons like Novak Djokovic, Carlos Alcaraz, Iga Świątek, and Serena Williams, whose legacies transcend the sport. With four distinct Grand Slams—Australian Open, Roland Garros, Wimbledon, US Open—each surface (hard, clay, grass) tests different skills, creating a unique spectacle.

🎾 Roger Federer — elegance and precision personified

Scoring is unique: 15, 30, 40, game. A player must win six games (by a margin of two) to take a set, and best-of-three or five sets decides the match. Tennis demands speed, agility, tactical intelligence, and relentless conditioning. From public parks to Centre Court, the spirit of tennis unites generations across Europe, Americas, Asia and beyond.

📜 HISTORY OF TENNIS

The origins trace back to 12th-century France, where a handball game called "jeu de paume" evolved. By the 16th century, racquets were introduced, and "royal tennis" gained popularity among European nobility. Modern lawn tennis was patented in 1874 by Major Walter Clopton Wingfield, who laid out the rules that resemble today’s game.

🏛️ Jeu de paume — the medieval predecessor of tennis

The All England Croquet Club held the first Wimbledon Championship in 1877, making it the oldest tennis tournament. The Open Era began in 1968, allowing professionals to compete alongside amateurs, sparking the golden age of tennis. Legends like Billie Jean King, Rod Laver, Martina Navratilova, Björn Borg, and later Pete Sampras, Steffi Graf, the Williams sisters, and the Big Three (Federer, Nadal, Djokovic) elevated the sport to global heights.

👑 Serena Williams — 23 Grand Slam singles titles, an icon of power

Women's tennis gained massive visibility through the WTA founded by Billie Jean King in 1973. Today, the Davis Cup, Billie Jean King Cup, and the four majors define the pinnacle of achievement. Tennis continues to evolve with modern racquet technology and analytics.

📏 OFFICIAL RULES & SCORING

Based on ITF (International Tennis Federation) regulations, tennis combines tradition with dynamic action.

🏟️ Court Dimensions & Surfaces

The court is a rectangle: 23.77m (78ft) long. For singles, width is 8.23m (27ft); doubles adds 1.37m alleys. The net is 0.914m (3ft) high at the center. Surfaces define gameplay: clay (slow, high bounce), grass (fast, low bounce), and hard court (medium pace, consistent).

📐 Official tennis court dimensions (ITF standard)

🎯 Scoring & Game Structure

🏅 Points progression
Love (0) → 15 → 30 → 40 → Game. Deuce occurs at 40-40; a player must win two consecutive points to secure the game.
📆 Sets & Matches
Standard matches are best-of-three sets (women, most men's events). Grand Slam men play best-of-five. Tiebreak at 6-6 (except final sets at some majors).
🔄 Serve rules
Players alternate serves each game. Two service attempts per point; a fault if the ball misses the service box. Double fault loses the point.
🚫 Let & Foot faults
A let (serve clips net but lands in) is replayed. Foot fault occurs if server steps on baseline before contact.

🏆 Key Rules & Line Calling

Balls landing on lines are considered "in." Players cannot touch the net or cross into opponent's side. Hawk-Eye electronic review is used in major tournaments to challenge line calls (3 challenges per set). In doubles, teamwork and positioning expand the court coverage. The ball must bounce only once per side, and volleys are allowed except on the serve.

🌟 Carlos Alcaraz — modern athleticism & powerful groundstrokes

Professional tennis also enforces time violations (25-second shot clock) and code violations for unsportsmanlike conduct. The let-cord rule on serve was modernized in some events with no-let on serves, but traditional let calls remain standard.

🛠️ TENNIS EQUIPMENT & SURFACES

From racquet innovation to specialized shoes, gear defines performance and style. Modern tennis blends high-tech materials with heritage aesthetics.

🎾 Racquet

Graphite composite frames, head sizes 95-110 sq in. Strings: polyester for spin, natural gut for feel. Weight, balance, and grip size customized for each player.

👟 Court Shoes

Herringbone tread for clay, durable outsoles for hard courts, and gentle grip for grass. Advanced cushioning and lateral support prevent injuries.

🎾 Tennis Balls

Pressurized felt-covered rubber balls. Type 1 (fast), Type 2 (medium, standard), Type 3 (slow). Balls are changed after 7-9 games in pro play.

🌱 Court Surfaces

Clay (Roland Garros): sliding, high topspin. Grass (Wimbledon): fast, low bounce. Hard (US Open/AO): balanced, speed and bounce consistency.

🧢 Apparel & Accessories

Moisture-wicking polos, skirts, and shorts engineered for breathability. Wristbands, headbands, and caps manage sweat. Advanced players use vibration dampeners, overgrips, and customized strings. Pro-level gear often includes sponsorship branding, and technology such as smart sensors track swing metrics.

🏆 Iga Świątek — dominant on clay, precision and intensity

Wearable tech like GPS trackers and heart rate monitors help athletes optimize training. Top players also rely on custom string tensions (typically 45-65 lbs) to balance power and control.
